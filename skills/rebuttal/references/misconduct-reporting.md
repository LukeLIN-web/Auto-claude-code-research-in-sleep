# Bad-faith, LLM-generated or abusive reviews: how to report

Read when an issue card carries `reviewer_stance: bad_faith_suspected` (Phase 2) or the user asks to report a reviewer.
A report is an outward-facing act on the user's paper: **draft it, show it, and let the user send it.** Never post it yourself.

Policy sources (checked 2026-11-09; re-check the venue's current guide before each use):
- ICLR 2027 Author Guidelines — https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines
- ICLR 2027 Reviewer Guidelines — https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/ReviewerGuidelines
- ICLR 2027 AI Policy for Reviewers — https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForReviewers
- ICLR 2027 Dates — https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/Dates
- ICLR 2026 response to LLM-generated papers and reviews — https://blog.iclr.cc/2025/11/19/iclr-2026-response-to-llm-generated-papers-and-reviews/
- ICLR 2026 retrospective — https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/03/31/a-retrospective-on-the-iclr-2026-review-process/
- NeurIPS 2025 Responsible Reviewing Initiative — https://blog.neurips.cc/2025/05/02/responsible-reviewing-initiative-for-neurips-2025/

## 1. Strict is not malicious — triage first

Reporting a reviewer who is merely tough wastes the AC's goodwill and makes the authors look thin-skinned. VQToken's Reviewer Gyx4 (rating 2) is the model of a **legitimate hard reviewer**: specific asks, re-read the rebuttal, replied twice with a sharper criterion, credited what was fixed. That reviewer is answered with argument ([rhetoric.md](rhetoric.md) § 3), never reported.

Reportable, with the venue's wording where it exists:

| Signal | Venue basis |
|---|---|
| False claims about what the paper contains or does (says a table/experiment/baseline is missing when it is there; attributes a claim the paper never makes) | ICLR 2026: "Very poor quality reviews that feature false claims, misrepresentations or hallucinated references are also a code of ethics violation" |
| Hallucinated references (cited papers that do not exist, or exist but say something else) | Same; ICLR 2026 authors "should post a confidential message to ACs and SACs pointing out the poor quality reviews and provide the necessary evidence" |
| Review generated by an LLM without engagement with the paper (generic text that fits any paper, questions answered in the paper's abstract, wrong method name) | ICLR 2027 AI policy: "Any LLM-generated falsehoods, hallucinations, or misrepresentations in reviews, or reviews that are otherwise low quality, are subject to disciplinary action" |
| Placeholder / content-free review | ICLR 2027 Reviewer Guide: "Low quality reviews will be flagged by ACs and SACs, and the flagged reviewers will be warned"; NeurIPS 2025: "ACs and SACs will flag any low-quality review, such as placeholder ones" |
| Hostile, personal or demeaning language | ICLR 2026 AC guide: ACs "moderate the discussions so that they are not toxic" |
| Pressure, threats, collusion attempts, attempts to identify authors | ICLR 2026 retrospective: offending members were "banned and desk rejected" |
| Breach of confidentiality (paper content shared or fed to a non-private tool) | ICLR 2026 LLM policy: confidentiality breaches "could result in consequences such as desk rejection of all of the reviewer's submissions" |

Gray zone — not reportable on its own; answer on the merits and mention to the AC only as part of a larger pattern:
- Requests to cite specific papers. Answer "We will cite X" if relevant. Only when the requested papers are unrelated to the submission and the request is a condition for the score is it worth noting to the AC, factually, without guessing who wrote them.
- Score inconsistent with the text (strengths only, rating 1–2). Point it out in the AC note as an observation, not an accusation.
- Silence after the rebuttal. Not misconduct; handled by [followup-and-endgame.md](followup-and-endgame.md).

Never a basis for a report:
- An LLM-detector score alone. ICLR uses detectors only for triage and acts "only … if an AC or SAC identifies concrete evidence"; its retrospective notes their "accuracy is far from perfect". A detector number can be one line in the evidence pack, after the factual errors.
- A guess at the reviewer's identity or affiliation.
- Disagreement on significance or taste.

## 2. Evidence pack

Build it in `rebuttal/MISCONDUCT_<ReviewerID>.md` (internal, never pasted anywhere as is). One row per claim:

| # | Reviewer text (verbatim) | What the paper actually says | Location | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "The paper does not compare with X." | Table 3 compares with X on 4 benchmarks | Table 3, l. 241 | false claim |
| 2 | "As shown in [Smith et al. 2024, CVPR] …" | No such paper in DBLP / CrossRef / Semantic Scholar / venue proceedings | — | hallucinated reference |
| 3 | "The method uses a diffusion prior." | The method has no diffusion component | §3 | misrepresentation |

Rules:
- Every row quotes the review verbatim and points to a line, section, table or figure of the **submitted** version (not the revised one).
- References are checked against DBLP → CrossRef → Semantic Scholar → the venue's proceedings; record each lookup.
- Count the rows. Two or three solid false claims or hallucinated references beat a long list of soft ones. Drop any row the paper does not settle unambiguously.
- Nothing in the pack admits a weakness of the paper (Never concede / Never expose a weakness apply here too). If a reviewer's point is partly right, it does not go in the pack; it gets a normal reply.

## 3. Channel and timing

**ICLR (2027 rules):**
1. **Confidential comment to the AC on the submission's forum.** Author Guidelines: "Authors who encounter potential violations of the Code of Ethics, e.g., as part of the review or public discussion, should raise these issues in a private message to their papers' Area Chair through the open review interface." Set *Readers* to the Area Chairs (and Senior Area Chairs / Program Chairs if offered); **never** include reviewers.
2. **Program Chairs by email**, only if the AC does not resolve it: "In cases that cannot be resolved together with the Area Chair, please contact the Program Chairs through email. Prefix your title as [Authors-to-PCs]." ICLR's policy posts list program-chairs@iclr.cc; confirm the address in the current guide.
3. **Timing.** ICLR 2027 author–reviewer discussion runs Nov 05 – Nov 18 '26 (AoE); reviewer–AC discussion and meta-reviewing begin Nov 19. Reviewers flag CoE concerns by Nov 18. Send the AC note as soon as the evidence pack is solid — early enough that the AC can still ask the reviewer to fix the review before reviewer–AC discussion starts. Do not wait for the reviewer to answer the rebuttal first.

**NeurIPS:** the 2025 reviewer acknowledgement states that "Area Chairs will be able to flag up Insufficient Reviews … [and] grossly irresponsible reviewers (including but not limited to possibly LLM-generated reviews)". Use the venue's AC-only comment, or the author note to the AC (VQToken's forum shows an "Author Final Remarks" note); check that year's author guide for which one exists.

**Other venues (CVPR/ICCV/ACL/…)**: find the AC-only / confidential comment in the author guide; if none exists, email the PCs with the same pack. Record the channel used in `REBUTTAL_STATE.md`.

## 4. The note to the AC

One message, factual, short (≈ 150–300 words plus the table). It asks the AC to weigh the review, not to punish anyone.

```
Title: Confidential note to the AC regarding Review <ID>

Dear Area Chair,

We would like to bring to your attention factual problems in Review <ID>
that bear on how it should be weighed. We have answered every point of the
review on the merits in the public thread; this note only records the
following verifiable issues.

1. The review states "<verbatim>". The submission reports <fact> in <location>.
2. The review cites "<reference>". We could not find this work in DBLP,
   CrossRef, Semantic Scholar or the <venue> proceedings.
3. <...>

[Optional, one line, only if true:] The review's text lists only strengths
("<verbatim>"), while the rating is <r>.

We respectfully ask that these points be taken into account when weighing
this review. We are happy to provide any further detail.

Authors of Submission <N>
```

Writing rules:
- Facts and locations only. No adjectives about the reviewer ("careless", "lazy", "malicious", "clearly AI"). The AC draws the conclusion.
- Do not write "LLM-generated" as a finding. If relevant, say "the review contains [N] references we could not locate and [M] statements that do not match the submission"; the venue's own policy text names the consequence.
- No request to remove the review or change the score. Ask that the points "be taken into account".
- No mention of other reviewers' scores, and no comparison with them, in this note (that belongs in the closing note, [followup-and-endgame.md](followup-and-endgame.md)).
- One note per reviewer. A follow-up only if new evidence appears (e.g. the reviewer's reply adds a new false claim) — not to chase the AC.

## 5. The public thread, at the same time

- Still answer every point of that review in the public thread, on the merits, with the same rhetoric rules as any reviewer. The public reply is what the AC and other reviewers read; it must hold on its own.
- In public, correct a false claim with the location, flatly: "Table 3 reports the comparison with X (4 benchmarks)." No "the reviewer did not read", no "misunderstanding", no hint that a report exists.
- Never write in public that a review is LLM-generated, hallucinated or in bad faith.

## 6. Never

- Contact a reviewer outside OpenReview, or try to identify one. After the ICLR 2026 identity leak, "collusion attempts, threats, and harassment" led to bans and desk rejections of the offenders' submissions.
- Pressure for a score, in public or in private.
- Post the report with reviewers among the readers.
- Send a report the user has not read and approved.
- Let the report replace the rebuttal: the paper is decided on the public answers; the report only reduces the weight of a review that cannot be answered.

## 7. Record

- `ISSUE_BOARD.md`: the reviewer's cards get `reviewer_stance: bad_faith_suspected` and a pointer to `MISCONDUCT_<ID>.md`; each card still needs a public answer (coverage gate).
- `REBUTTAL_STATE.md`: date sent, channel, readers setting, AC response (if any).
